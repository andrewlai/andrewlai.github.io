Ovarian cancer is usually diagnosed late, when survival is poor, and the main blood test in use, CA-125, misses many early cancers. Extracellular vesicles (EVs) released by tumour cells carry proteins, lipids and RNA that reflect the tissue they came from, which makes them a promising source of earlier and more specific markers.

This program profiles plasma EVs from patients and matched controls to find and verify those markers:

- **Proteomics in pre-diagnostic samples.** Using immunoaffinity-enriched EV proteomics, we have examined high-grade serous ovarian cancer in a nested case-control analysis of the UK Ovarian Cancer Population Study {% cite salomon2026ukops %}.
- **Lipidomics.** Small EV and total lipid profiles outperformed CA-125 across ovarian cancer subtypes {% cite rani2026sevlipid %}, and EV lipidomics reveals metabolic reprogramming in both the tumour and the host {% cite rani2026lipidomics %}.
- **Rapid detection platforms.** With materials science collaborators, we have developed mesoporous gold SERS biosensors that detect ovarian cancer-derived EVs directly {% cite RN76 hasan2026mesoporous %}.
- **Treatment response.** EV cargo also tracks chemoresistance and recurrence {% cite RN45 RN53 %}.

A key outcome has been contributing to **OCRF-7**, an early-detection blood test for ovarian cancer that is now in clinical validation.
