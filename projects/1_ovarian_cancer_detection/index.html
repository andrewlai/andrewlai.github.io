Ovarian cancer early detection

Blood-based extracellular vesicle biomarkers for detecting ovarian cancer earlier

Ovarian cancer is usually diagnosed late, when survival is poor, and the main blood test in use, CA-125, misses many early cancers. Extracellular vesicles (EVs) released by tumour cells carry proteins, lipids and RNA that reflect the tissue they came from, which makes them a promising source of earlier and more specific markers.

This program profiles plasma EVs from patients and matched controls to find and verify those markers:

  • Proteomics in pre-diagnostic samples. Using immunoaffinity-enriched EV proteomics, we have examined high-grade serous ovarian cancer in a nested case-control analysis of the UK Ovarian Cancer Population Study (Salomon et al., 2026).
  • Lipidomics. Small EV and total lipid profiles outperformed CA-125 across ovarian cancer subtypes (Rani et al., 2026), and EV lipidomics reveals metabolic reprogramming in both the tumour and the host (Rani et al., 2026).
  • Rapid detection platforms. With materials science collaborators, we have developed mesoporous gold SERS biosensors that detect ovarian cancer-derived EVs directly (Bashir et al., 2025; Hasan et al., 2026).
  • Treatment response. EV cargo also tracks chemoresistance and recurrence (Alharbi et al., 2020; Alharbi† et al., 2021).

A key outcome has been contributing to OCRF-7, an early-detection blood test for ovarian cancer that is now in clinical validation.
